# Week 7 · Image and 3D Generation

Prepare before **Wednesday 21 October 2026**.

Next class is Image and 3D Generation. Last class (14 October) showed the
process on the Lingnan gateway. Now do it with a subject of your own.

**Mention your difficulties.**

## 1. Choose an object with a history

Choose **at least one** object; more are welcome. It can be anything: a person,
a building, an animal, a thing.

It must have **a history**. That can mean:

- an earlier stage of it: a person ten or twenty years ago, a building before
  it changed, or further back;
- or an event in history that it was part of.

Find the sources you need: photographs, descriptions, records. Keep them, with
where each came from.

## 2. Generate an image of it

Generate an image of your object at the stage or event you chose.

Then show **how yours differs from what anyone else could make**. Someone with
no sources and no research can type a description and get an image too. Make
that image as well, from words alone, and put the two side by side:

- What does your image get right that theirs gets wrong or leaves vague?
- Which sources made the difference, and how did you use them?
- What did the generator still invent, in both?

## 3. Turn it into 3D

Make a 3D model of your object. Use any route from last class: an agent
building it in Blender, an image-to-3D service, or both. If one part needs more
detail, you may make it on its own and fit it back.

**If your computer cannot run Blender**, use a school computer, or skip Blender
and use only an image-to-3D service and an image generator. That is enough for
this task.

## 4. Then do what you like with it

Use your model however suits your project: part of a game, part of an
environment, a demonstration video, an animation, a generated video, or
something else. Your choice.

## Tools you can choose

You are not limited to the tools we used. Try others and say which you chose.

- **Image-to-3D:** we used Tripo (new accounts get some free credits; it
  accepts PayPal). Alternatives include Tencent Hunyuan3D, Meshy and Rodin.
- **Image generation:** we used Google's Nano Banana 2. Alternatives include
  OpenAI's GPT Image, ByteDance's Seedream and Black Forest Labs' Flux.

Check what a service costs before you spend, and keep the receipt.

## Bring to class

- Your repository link, with an index.
- Your sources, and the two images side by side with your answers to step 2.
- Your 3D model: screenshots, or a short screen recording.
- What you made with it in step 4.
- What each tool did, what the AI did and what you checked yourself.
- At least one real difficulty, with its example.

Keep every prompt, input, output and cost. Keep large files out of Git: put
model files over a few MB in `artifacts/`, which Git already ignores, or store
them elsewhere and link them. Keep API keys out of Git. Put unresolved questions
in `questions.md`.
